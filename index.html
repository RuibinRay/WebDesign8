    The Horae

    Before the hours were numbers, they had names.

    The Greeks gave the day twelve keepers, the Horae: the sisters who opened the gates of the sky at dawn and closed them at night. Auge was first light. Mesembria was noon. Arktos was the night, named for the Bear, the stars that never set.

    We build each watch around one of them: its light, its colour, the length of its shadow. Four so far. The other eight are on the rotor of every movement, engraved by hand.
